# AISG | My Learners — Real Teacher Usability Study Protocol
**Status:** Facilitator-ready protocol. **No human participants have been tested in this development audit.**

## Study overview
Recruit **10 AISG educators** with varied teaching experience and confidence with digital tools: PK3, Kindergarten, Grade 2, Grade 4, Grade 5, Grade 7 Mathematics, Grade 8 Language & Literature, Grade 9 Science, Grade 10 Individuals & Societies, and Grade 12 DP. Participation should be voluntary and should not be connected to appraisal or teacher evaluation. Run each session individually (25–35 minutes), with an impartial facilitator and observer when possible.

**Purpose:** Observe whether a teacher can independently understand the homepage, navigate across student/class data, find evidence, make an instructional response, and recover their original context. Use a **synthetic demonstration** only; do not ask participants to enter, identify, or disclose real student information.

## Introduction & consent (read aloud)
> We are testing the My Learners website, **not your ability to use technology**. You can stop at any time. The site contains fictional students and fictional teacher–class assignments. I will ask you to complete several tasks without showing you how. Please say what you are looking for or expecting as you move around. We will record the steps, any confusion, and your voluntary feedback to improve the tool. With your permission we may record the screen, but not confidential school information or personal student information. May we proceed? May we record the screen?

Record separate Yes/No responses for participation and screen recording, explain where recordings will be stored and when destroyed under AISG-approved research/data policies. Do not take recordings without affirmative consent.

## Five common tasks — read exactly, without hints

| # | Teacher task | Expected evidence of completion | Suggested design target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | “You are teaching next. Find the class and tell me one piece of information that would help you prepare.” | Opens next class, interprets a recent information card | <30 s |
| 2 | “Find a learner in that class. Explore their most recent learning evidence, then go back to the class where you started.” | Learner profile, formative evidence, exact class return | <60 s |
| 3 | “Find what classroom guidance you should consider for a student, and explain where that guidance came from.” | Support section, appropriate source/limitations; no assumptions | <30 s |
| 4 | “Explore this learner's MAP growth **if available**. Explain what observed growth and projected growth mean, and find RIT if you need more context.” | Correctly identifies growth, period and supporting evidence; identifies not assessed where relevant | <60 s |
| 5 | “Record one brief response to the learning evidence. Set a follow-up, then return to your original class.” | Demo action submitted, followed up or revisited, navigates back | <60 s |

Targets are **proposed**, not measured teacher task times. Record actual time from the end of each prompt to the participant's final response.

## Persona-specific follow-up
- PK3 / Kindergarten: Is evidence phrased meaningfully for early childhood? Is MAP absent when inappropriate?
- Grade 2: Is developmental/formative evidence intuitive? Can you navigate without assistance?
- Grade 4–5: Does MAP growth meaningfully connect with recent classroom learning? Can you edit flexible group suggestions?
- Grade 7 / Grade 9: Switch teaching groups and return to a filtered roster; are class transitions obvious?
- Grade 8 / Grade 10: Find relevant subject criteria and assignment status; revisit an action.
- Grade 12: Are DP-style assessment details prominent and age-appropriate rather than empty MAP summaries?

## Final interview (5 minutes)
1. “Would you voluntarily open this before class? Why or why not?”
2. “Which information felt most useful for an instructional decision?”
3. “Was anything unclear, distracting, or hard to return from?”
4. “Was there a moment you expected the interface to do something different?”
5. “What one change would make this more useful **tomorrow**?”
6. “How confident would you feel using it without assistance?” 1–5.
7. “Which school systems would this genuinely replace checking, and which must remain open?”

## Analysis and decision gates
- Separate **observed failures** from **teacher opinions** and **facilitator inference**.
- Report task success counts and medians only after actual participation.
- Aggregate responses; avoid identifying individual teachers in reports without explicit agreement.
- Prioritise P0 (safety/scope/data loss), P1 (core journey blocked), P2 (time/clarity), P3 (cosmetic).
- Re-test changed workflows before the actual student-data integration pilot.
- Return anonymised outcomes and intended improvements to participating teachers.

## Accessibility add-on
Invite at least one participant who regularly uses keyboard navigation, assistive technology, increased browser zoom or small-screen devices, if they freely elect to participate. Run selected tasks at 200% zoom and keyboard-only. Do not infer disability or collect health history from participants.

## Ethical boundary
The public app includes **real AISG faculty names but fictional student identities and class assignments**. Do not treat the synthetic teacher assignments as an official timetable or enter confidential student information into browser-local demo follow-ups. Live AISG data and legal/data-residency reviews are outside this research protocol.
